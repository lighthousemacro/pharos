The mental model you have is the wrong one

01You are not a bookmaker. You are the track.

This is the whole misunderstanding. "How do I pay winners" and "where's my liquidity" are bookmaker questions. We did not build a bookmaker.

A sportsbook (what you pictured)

bettor↔ THE HOUSE (you)

The house is the counterparty to every bet. A winning bettor is paid out of the house's own bankroll. If sharps keep winning, the house bleeds. This needs a huge balance sheet and it is the model you correctly do not want.

Needs: a bankroll the size of the action. Risk: unbounded. This is not Pharos.

A pari-mutuel pool (what Pharos is)

losers' stakes → THE POT → winners
you → run the pot, take a rake

Every dollar bet is locked in that market's pot. Winners are paid out of the losers' stakes, not your pocket. You operate the pot and take a cut of what flows through. The contract literally cannot pay out more than it holds (this is the solvency invariant already tested in the code).

Needs: a small seed to start a market. Risk: only that seed, and only you choose it. This is Pharos.

Your strongest objection, answered

03"In what world do I have that liquidity?"

You don't need it. That worry is the bookmaker model again. Three true things:

Fact 1
The seed is tiny

A market needs just enough to quote a starting price. On Arc right now your live markets are seeded with $1 each and they work. A market can do $1M of volume on a $500 seed. Your money does not scale with the action.

Fact 2
The seed is your dial

You set it per market. $1, $100, $1,000. That number is your maximum loss on that market if the framework is wrong. It is bounded, chosen by you, and never an open tab.

Fact 3
You need not fund depth at all

At scale, outside liquidity providers fund the pools for a share of the rake (how every DEX works), or the crowd funds both sides. You operate the venue. You are not the bank.

The honest catch: thin pools mean clunky early UX and a cold start. The fix is the next section. The thing that pulls the first liquidity in is the second number.

The actual question

04Why a second number is the whole business (and is not "we make you win")

You sensed the trap yourself. If the second number just made everyone win, everyone piles one side, the price snaps to the framework, and the edge is gone in one trade. Nobody wins repeatedly. So that is not what it does. Here is what it does.

US CPI prints hot (≥ 3%)?
crowd 55%
framework 83%

It is a benchmark, not a tip

A stock with no analyst coverage versus a stock with a rated, track-recorded research note attached. People do not pay for research because it is always right. They pay because it is a consistent, accountable reference that makes their own call sharper. The framework number is that, bolted onto a live bet, with a public scorecard. The crowd number alone is just noise with a dollar sign.

You sell the venue, not their P&L

Vegas posts a model line next to the public betting percentage. That line does not make bettors rich. It makes that the place serious money watches and bets. You are selling the watering hole, not the water. Your revenue is the rake on flow. More credible venue, more flow, more rake. The bettor's edge is the bettor's business.

— and here is why disagreement never dies, so the market never kills itself —

Macro prediction markets are thin and narrative-driven. People fade models constantly, that is why they bet. The framework price also moves with every new print, so there is no fixed answer to converge to. And the pricing curve self-limits any whale: the more they push one side, the worse their price gets, until there is nothing left to win. Disagreement is perpetual because humans and new data are perpetual. That is the engine, and it does not run down.

So what are you, concretely

06The three identities, on one page

Same asset, the framework. Three ways to get paid by it. The difference is who provides liquidity and how the money reaches you.

Option 1 · built & live
Be the market

You run the venue. Liquidity: a tiny seed you choose, plus the crowd and outside LPs. You get paid: the rake on all flow, always, plus a bounded seed position. The second number is the draw that brings the flow.

Option 2 · the LHM-native one
Sell the number

You do not run a venue. You publish the framework price on contracts that already exist elsewhere. Liquidity: none, not your problem. You get paid: subscription / API. It is your research business, real-time, on tradable contracts.

Option 3 · park it
Trade the gap

You trade the edge yourself with capital. Liquidity: your capital, capped by thin market depth. You get paid: P&L. Purest, but capacity-limited and the heaviest to do compliantly solo.

For the hackathon you are Option 1, because that is the brief and it is what is deployed on Arc. The hackathon is a free, funded prototype that lets you test the most expensive option without committing the company. Which one Lighthouse becomes is a real decision, and it is yours, after the 25th.

No selling you

07The real risks, said plainly

  • Seed loss. If the framework is wrong on a market, the seed you put up there can be taken. Bounded by your choice per market, never more. The conviction throttle already built makes the framework seed thin exactly where it is unsure.
  • Cold start. Thin early liquidity is real and is the weak axis in the hackathon scoring too. The answer is distribution: your existing macro audience is the first crowd.
  • Brand and compliance. You are a research firm, not an RIA, and putting the Lighthouse name on a betting venue with "+EV" language has an angle worth thinking through. That is a real conversation, and it is for after the deadline.

The one paragraph, if you read nothing else. You are not a bookmaker, so you never cover bets and you never need a bankroll. You run the pot and take the rake. The seed is tiny, bounded, and your choice. The second number is not a tip that makes people win, it is the benchmark that makes your venue the credible one, which is what brings the flow you rake. A 1-number market is gambling. A 2-number market is gambling plus quants plus contrarians plus an audience plus a self-running scorecard. You are selling the watering hole. The framework is why anyone walks to yours.
